# What Information Should Be Captured When a Bank Valuation Assignment Is Received?

A site engineer leaves the office at ten in the morning for a property on the other side of the city. He has the borrower's name and a rough locality. He does not have a landmark. The borrower's phone is switched off. The exact village was never written down — it was somewhere in a WhatsApp message that has now scrolled up past a hundred others.

By one o'clock he is standing at the wrong end of a panchayat, calling the office to ask which lane. Half a day is gone before a single photograph is taken.

Almost every valuer has lived some version of this. And here is the uncomfortable part: the day was lost before the site visit even started. It was lost at intake.

## Intake is the cheapest stage to get right

Every stage of a valuation has a cost of error. A wrong rate may get caught in review. A bad photo can sometimes be retaken. But a missing landmark or a wrong phone number does its damage early and quietly, and the cost of fixing it later is always higher than the cost of capturing it correctly at the start.

Capturing a borrower's alternate number when the file arrives takes ten seconds. Discovering you need it when the site engineer is stranded costs a wasted trip. Intake is the one stage where a few minutes of discipline saves hours downstream.

## What weak intake actually causes

When intake is rushed, the same problems repeat:

- Site visits to the wrong location
- Repeated calls to the branch for details that should have been captured once
- Borrowers asked the same questions twice, which looks unprofessional
- Files that cannot be told apart because three of them are "the Sharma file"
- Documents that were "received" but no one can locate

None of these are dramatic. They are small, daily, and they add up to a slower office.

## The details worth capturing at intake

Group them into five simple sets. Not every field applies to every assignment, and the exact list depends on the bank or office process — but this is a practical starting point.

### Assignment identity

- **Internal assignment reference** — a short code the office assigns, so the file has a name from day one
- **Assignment received date** — when it actually came in, which is where every timeline starts

The reference matters more than it looks. Without one, files get nicknamed — "the bypass-road one," "Kumar's file," "the urgent HDFC one." Then a second Kumar arrives, and a second urgent HDFC file, and the office is one confused phone call away from mixing them up.

### Bank and branch details

- **Bank name**
- **Branch name**
- **Bank contact**, where available — the relationship manager or branch official who sent the work

The branch contact is who you go back to when the borrower cannot be reached. Capturing it at the start means you are not hunting for "who sent us this" three days later.

### Borrower and contact details

- **Borrower name**
- **Co-borrower name**, where applicable
- **Borrower or contact phone number**
- **Alternate contact**, where available

A single phone number is a single point of failure. The borrower travels, the phone dies, the number is entered wrong by one digit. An alternate contact — a spouse, a relative, the person who actually has the keys — is often the difference between a completed visit and a wasted one.

### Property location details

- **Property address**
- **Landmark**
- **Locality / village / town**
- **Taluk**
- **District**
- **State**

This is where most failed visits are born. In large parts of the country, a postal address alone will not get a site engineer to the right gate — the landmark does. It is also worth noting at intake when the survey or plot number on the document may not visibly match what is painted on site, so the engineer is not left guessing at the location.

### Property and document details

- **Property type** — land, residential, commercial, industrial, and so on
- **Purpose of valuation**, where known
- **Documents received** — listed specifically, not just "documents received"
- **Documents pending** — so the gap is visible from the start

The phrase "documents received" with no list is one of the quiet traps of valuation work. Weeks later, when a query comes, no one can say what was actually handed over. Listing each document — and listing what is still pending — turns a vague note into something the office can act on.

### Responsibility and timeline

- **Site visit assigned to whom**
- **Due date or expected timeline**
- **Notes / special instructions**

Special instructions are where memory fails most often. "Borrower only available Sundays." "Society gate closes by 6." "Approach road dug up, go via the temple side." These belong in the file, not in the head of whoever took the call — because that person may not be the one who does the visit.

## Why intake should not live only in WhatsApp

WhatsApp is genuinely useful for receiving assignments quickly. The problem is not WhatsApp; it is that a chat is a stream, not a record. Details scroll away. The same thread carries assignments, photos, jokes, and follow-ups all mixed together. When intake lives only in chat, finding a borrower's village three weeks later means scrolling through a hundred messages — and hoping it was typed at all.

The fix is small: whatever arrives by chat or call gets transferred once into a structured intake — a form, a sheet, a register — where it stops being a passing message and becomes the file's permanent foundation. (This is the same pattern explored in *Why Valuation Offices Still Run on WhatsApp and Excel — and What It Quietly Costs* — the tools are fine, but a chat stream is not a system of record.)

## Why every assignment should have a reference from the start

A reference number is the smallest piece of intake and one of the most useful. It gives the file an identity the whole office can use — on the tracker, on the photo folder, on the document set, in conversation. "Where is VR-204" is a question anyone can answer. "Where is the Kumar file" depends on which Kumar, and on whose memory you are asking. Give the file a name on day one, and everything attached to it later — photos, drafts, corrections, the final report — has something to hang on.

## FAQs

**Does every bank require all of these intake details?**
No. Requirements vary by bank and by office process, and this checklist is a practical starting point, not a universal rule. The point is to capture what your own work actually needs — the fields that, when missing, cause failed visits and repeated calls.

**Why is an internal reference number important?**
Because it gives every file a name from day one. Without it, files get nicknamed by borrower or locality, which breaks down the moment two similar assignments arrive. A short internal code keeps files distinct across the whole office.

**Is it really necessary to capture an alternate contact?**
It is one of the highest-value fields at intake. A single phone number is a single point of failure — switched off, travelling, or entered wrong. An alternate contact often saves an entire site visit.

**Should documents be listed individually or just marked "received"?**
Individually. "Documents received" with no list becomes useless weeks later when a query arrives and no one can say what was actually provided. Listing each document — and each pending one — keeps the gap visible.

**Can intake be done over WhatsApp?**
Assignments often arrive over WhatsApp, and that is fine. The issue is leaving intake there. A chat is a stream where details scroll away; intake should be transferred once into a structured place so it becomes a permanent part of the file.
