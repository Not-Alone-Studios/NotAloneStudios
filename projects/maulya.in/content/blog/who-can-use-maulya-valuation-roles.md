## A valuation assignment is not handled by one screen and one person

A valuation assignment is not handled by one screen and one person. It moves through people.

Most software written for valuers imagines a single user at a desk, typing a report. But the real work rarely happens that way. An assignment comes in, someone visits the site, someone collects photos and documents, someone prepares the draft, and the valuer reviews and signs off. That is four or five different hands on one file — and each hand needs to see something different.

Maulya is being built around those roles, not around a single screen. Here is how each one fits.

## Maulya for the solo valuer

Plenty of valuers run the whole show themselves — intake, visit, report, sign-off. The challenge is not knowledge; it is holding the state of every active assignment in your head while new ones keep arriving.

For a solo valuer, Maulya is being designed to keep that state outside your head: which assignments are open, which site visits are pending, where evidence is, and which reports are waiting to be finalized. The aim is less mental tracking and fewer "did I forget something?" moments — so attention goes to the valuation, not to remembering the queue.

## Maulya for the valuation firm owner

For a firm owner, the recurring question is simple and hard to answer quickly: *what is the status of everything right now?*

Maulya is being built to give that visibility across files — who is holding what, which assignments are pending, which are waiting on a site visit, and which reports are in review or awaiting approval. The aim is not to watch over people; it is to stop work from going quietly stuck because no one had the full picture. An owner should be able to see the office's load without calling three people to assemble it.

## Maulya for the site engineer

The site engineer's work is specific: go to the right property, capture what is needed, and get it back cleanly.

Maulya is being designed so a site engineer can see assigned site visits, the borrower contact and address for each, and what evidence is expected — and then upload photos and documents against the correct assignment. Site notes and pending items (a measurement to confirm, a document still to collect) can stay attached to the file instead of living in a separate chat. The point is to make handing work back to the office clean, not improvised.

## Maulya for office staff

Office staff are often the quiet engine of a valuation practice — chasing details, preparing drafts, tracking what is missing.

Maulya is being built to support exactly that: seeing assignment details without re-asking the valuer, finding the site evidence attached to a file, knowing the document status, supporting report preparation, and tracking correction and approval status. The goal is fewer repeated questions and fewer "where is that photo?" interruptions, so the office runs on a shared record rather than constant back-and-forth.

## Maulya for junior valuers and assistants

A junior learns the trade by seeing how good work flows. When the workflow lives in one person's head, that learning is slow and the same questions get asked again and again.

Maulya is being designed so a structured workflow is visible — what comes after intake, what a site visit should produce, what a report needs before review. That structure helps a junior learn the rhythm of an assignment and reduces repeated questions, without pretending the junior is doing the valuer's job.

## What Maulya does *not* do for these roles

This is the important part, and it is deliberate.

Maulya does **not** make a site engineer responsible for valuation judgment. Capturing evidence is not forming a valuation opinion.

Maulya does **not** make office staff responsible for the professional valuation opinion. Preparing and organizing a report is not signing off on its conclusions.

And Maulya does **not** remove valuer review. The valuer's review and final approval stay in place. No role-based screen changes who is professionally responsible for the valuation — that remains the valuer.

## Why role clarity matters

When everyone shares one undifferentiated chat thread, two things go wrong. Work falls between people because no one is clearly the owner of the next step, and people see noise that is not theirs to act on. Role clarity fixes both: each person sees the work they need to handle, and the next step always has an owner. That is quieter to run and far easier to scale than a single group where everything is everyone's — which usually means no one's.

## One assignment, moving through roles

Picture a single bank valuation assignment.

It arrives and is captured at intake with the bank, branch, borrower, and property details. The firm owner sees a new file in the queue. It is assigned for a site visit; the site engineer sees the address and contact, visits, and uploads photos and documents against that assignment, with a note that one approval document is still pending. Office staff pick it up, see the evidence already attached, prepare the draft, and flag the missing document. The valuer reviews the inputs and draft, applies professional judgment, asks for a correction, and then gives final approval. The completed file is stored where it can be found again.

Same assignment, five roles, one shared record. Nobody had to reconstruct the file from messages, and at every moment it was clear who owned the next step.

## Practical summary

- A valuation assignment moves through several roles, not one user at a desk.
- **Solo valuer:** keep the queue out of your head.
- **Firm owner:** see status across all files.
- **Site engineer:** see assigned visits, capture and upload evidence cleanly.
- **Office staff:** find details and evidence, prepare reports, track corrections.
- **Junior/assistant:** learn a visible workflow, ask fewer repeat questions.
- Maulya does **not** shift valuation responsibility — review and sign-off stay with the valuer.

## FAQs

**1. Is Maulya only for large valuation firms?**
No. It is being built for solo valuers and small firms as much as larger offices. A solo valuer plays several of these roles at once.

**2. Can a site engineer or office staff member finalize a valuation in Maulya?**
No. They handle evidence, details, and report preparation. The valuation opinion, review, and final approval remain with the valuer.

**3. Are these roles and permissions final?**
Maulya's MVP is still under development. Role behaviour is being built and will be confirmed as the product is released.

**4. Does each person need a separate login?**
Exact access and account details will be confirmed at release. The design intent is that each role sees the work relevant to them.

**5. Will Maulya tell me what my staff are doing every minute?**
That is not the intent. The goal is visibility into where work stands, so files don't get stuck — not minute-by-minute monitoring of people.
