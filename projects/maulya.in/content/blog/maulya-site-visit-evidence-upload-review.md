Site photos are useful only when everyone knows which assignment they belong to.

Any valuation office that has run for a while has felt this problem. A site engineer visits four properties in a day, takes sixty or seventy photos across all of them, and by evening the phone gallery is one long scroll of buildings, gates, rooms, and documents with no labels. When office staff sit down to prepare a draft, the first job isn't valuation — it's detective work. Which photos belong to which property? Was this the front elevation for the Anekal site or the Hoskote one? Where is the approach road photo?

Evidence that nobody can confidently connect to an assignment becomes hard to use.

This page explains how Maulya is being built to support site visit evidence upload and review, and where its limits are.

## Why site evidence matters

A bank valuation report rests on what was actually seen at the property. The photos, document images, and observations about access and occupancy support the report. If that evidence is scattered, three things suffer: the draft takes longer to prepare, the valuer has less to review, and months later the file is almost impossible to reconstruct.

The problem is rarely that engineers don't take enough photos. It is that the photos live in a personal gallery and travel through WhatsApp, detached from the assignment they describe.

## What Maulya is being built to support

Maulya is being built to help site engineers and offices upload site evidence against the correct assignment, so it stays connected to the file it belongs to. Depending on the workflow, the evidence Maulya is designed to support includes:

- **Site photos** — elevation, structure, surroundings
- **Document images** captured at the site
- **Site notes and observations**
- **Measurements and observations**, where applicable
- **Occupancy notes** — occupied, vacant, tenanted, as observed
- **Approach and access notes**
- **Boundary observations**
- **Missing or pending items** — anything that still needs to be collected

The intent is that when an engineer finishes a visit, the photos and notes are already attached to the right assignment, not sitting in a gallery waiting to be sorted.

## Why evidence has to be tied to the assignment reference

This is the core idea, so it is worth stating plainly. A photo on its own is just an image. A photo linked to an assignment reference is part of a file. The link is what makes the evidence usable.

When evidence is tied to the reference:

- Office staff can open one assignment and see everything from the visit
- The valuer reviews a more complete file instead of asking for missing photos
- Pending items are visible, so gaps get closed before the report is drafted, not after
- Old files stay retrievable, because the evidence never got separated from the assignment

## What Maulya does not do

Being honest about the limits matters here, because site evidence is a sensitive area.

Maulya does not prove ownership. A photo shows what a property looks like; it does not establish who owns it.

It does not automatically validate documents. An uploaded document image is stored and linked — it is not checked for authenticity or accuracy by the platform.

It does not detect fraud. Maulya is a workflow tool for organising evidence, not a fraud-detection system.

It does not make GPS tags or photos legally conclusive. Location data and timestamps may support an office's own records, but Maulya does not treat them as legal proof, and neither should anyone relying on the file.

And it does not replace site inspection judgment. The engineer's and valuer's professional observations at the property remain the real basis of the assessment. Maulya is built to carry that evidence cleanly, not to make the judgment for anyone.

## Scattered evidence vs assignment-linked evidence

**Scattered:** Photos from four sites sit together in one gallery. Some are sent on WhatsApp, some aren't. Office staff message the engineer to ask which photos go where, and wait. A boundary photo is missing, but nobody notices until the valuer asks during review — by which time the engineer has moved on to other visits.

**Assignment-linked:** Each property's photos, notes, and document images are uploaded against its own assignment. Office staff open the file and the visit is already there. A pending item — say, a missing rear boundary photo — is flagged on the assignment, so it gets collected on a nearby follow-up instead of forcing a repeat trip.

The work the engineer does at the site is the same. What changes is whether that work survives the journey back to the office intact.

Maulya's evidence workflow is intended to make site visit handover cleaner: photos, documents, notes, and pending items connected to the right file.

## Practical summary

- Evidence is only useful when it's connected to the right assignment — a gallery of unlabelled photos is the slow part.
- Maulya is being built to let engineers and offices upload photos, document images, notes, occupancy/access/boundary observations, and pending items against the correct assignment.
- Tying evidence to the assignment reference is what makes it reviewable today and retrievable later.
- Maulya does **not** prove ownership, validate documents, detect fraud, or make GPS/photos legally conclusive.
- The site inspection judgment stays with the engineer and valuer; Maulya carries the evidence, it doesn't form the opinion.

## FAQs

**Q1. Can site engineers upload photos directly against an assignment in Maulya?**
That is how the evidence workflow is being designed — so photos and notes attach to the correct assignment rather than living in a personal gallery. As Maulya's MVP is still in development, this page describes the intended workflow.

**Q2. Does Maulya check whether a property document photo is genuine?**
No. Maulya stores and links the image to the assignment. It does not authenticate or validate documents. That review remains a professional task.

**Q3. Are GPS-tagged photos treated as legal proof in Maulya?**
No. Location data and timestamps may help an office keep clearer records, but Maulya does not present them as legally conclusive, and they should not be relied on that way.

**Q4. Does Maulya decide if a site visit was done correctly?**
No. Maulya helps organise what was captured. Whether the inspection was adequate is the judgment of the engineer and valuer.

**Q5. Are photos mandatory for every bank assignment in Maulya?**
Maulya does not impose bank requirements. What evidence is needed depends on the assignment and the office's process. Maulya is being built to hold whatever evidence the office captures, cleanly linked to the file.
