## Maulya starts with a workflow problem, not a valuation problem

Maulya exists because most valuation offices do not have a valuation problem first. They have a workflow visibility problem.

Ask any working valuer where a particular assignment stands right now — the one the branch called about yesterday — and the honest answer is usually a small investigation. Check WhatsApp. Check the Excel sheet. Ask the office staff. Ask whether the site engineer went. Open three folders. The valuation knowledge is rarely the bottleneck. Finding the current state of the work is.

Maulya is being built to reduce that gap.

## What Maulya actually is

Maulya is a workflow platform being built for Indian valuation offices. It is designed to hold a bank valuation assignment in one place from the moment it arrives — through site visit, evidence collection, valuation inputs, report preparation, internal review, and final storage — so the office can see where each file stands and who owns the next step.

That is the whole intention. Maulya is not being built to produce a value on its own, and it is not a replacement for a registered or bank-approved valuer. The professional judgment stays with the valuer. Maulya is being built to organize the work *around* that judgment, not to take it over.

## The problem most valuation offices already recognize

If you run or work in a valuation office, this pattern will feel familiar.

A bank assignment arrives — sometimes as a forwarded message, sometimes a phone call, sometimes a scan. Borrower contact and address sit in WhatsApp. Pending work lives in an Excel sheet that one person updates. Site photos sit in a phone gallery, mixed with personal pictures, until someone forwards them. Office staff keep asking the valuer the same questions — *which property, which branch, did the site visit happen, where are the documents.* A few reports wait on the valuer's desk for review. And six months later, when a bank or borrower asks for an old report, the file is hard to retrieve because it is scattered across a laptop, a phone, an email thread, and a printout.

None of this is a sign of a bad office. It is what happens when work moves through messages and memory instead of a shared, visible place. It works — until volume rises, until a junior joins, until two assignments collide on the same day.

## Why Maulya exists

The belief behind Maulya is simple: valuers do not need more hustle. They need better systems.

Many tools and office habits in valuation work focus mainly on report preparation or formatting. They make the document, but they leave the *office* — the tracking, the follow-ups, the evidence, the pending list — exactly where it was. Maulya is being built to address the part that quietly eats time and attention: keeping the assignment itself visible from intake to final file.

## What Maulya is being built to help with

Maulya is being designed to support a real assignment lifecycle, not a generic task list. Depending on the office workflow, that includes:

- **Assignment intake** — capturing a new assignment in one place instead of across messages.
- **Bank, branch, borrower, and property details** — the basics an office re-asks for repeatedly.
- **Site visit tracking** — whether the visit is pending, scheduled, or done.
- **Evidence upload** — keeping site photos and documents attached to the right assignment.
- **Valuation inputs recorded by the valuer** — observations and figures kept with the file.
- **Report preparation workflow** — moving a file from inputs toward a draft.
- **Correction and review** — making the review step visible instead of a desk pile.
- **Final approval** — the valuer's sign-off as a clear step, not an assumption.
- **Final report storage** — so a completed file can be found later without a search.

These are the stages of work an office already does. Maulya is being built to make them visible in one place.

## What Maulya is not

It is worth being just as clear about the boundaries.

Maulya is **not a borrower-facing valuation calculator.** It is not meant for property buyers or borrowers to estimate a value.

It is **not an automatic property-value engine.** It does not produce a figure on its own, and it is not being positioned as "AI valuation."

It is **not a replacement for a registered or bank-approved valuer.** Inspection, method, rates, adjustments, and the final opinion remain the valuer's professional responsibility.

It is **not a legal or compliance guarantee.** It does not make claims about title, ownership, or construction approvals, and it is not a substitute for professional and statutory obligations.

And it is **not a generic CRM** bent to fit valuation. It is being built around how valuation offices actually move work.

## Why workflow clarity matters for Indian valuation offices

Valuation work in India is distributed by nature. An assignment may involve a branch, a borrower, a site visit in a taluk or panchayat area, a site engineer, office staff preparing the report, and the valuer reviewing and signing off. That is several hands on one file.

When the current state of that file lives only in someone's head or a chat thread, three things happen: work waits without anyone noticing, the same questions get asked again, and old files become hard to recover. A shared, visible record of where each assignment stands is the quiet thing that keeps an office calm as it grows.

## Who can benefit

Maulya is being built for the office side of valuation work — solo valuers who want to stop tracking everything in their head, small valuation firm owners who want visibility across files, site engineers handling site visits and evidence, and office staff who prepare reports and chase pending items. It is not built for borrowers, property buyers, or investors.

## An honest closing

Maulya is still under development, and this page is being written before the product fully launches. So the honest framing is this: Maulya's goal is to help a valuation office know where every assignment stands, who owns the next step, and what is pending — without replacing the valuer's professional judgment.

That is a smaller promise than "AI that values property." It is also a more useful one for the people who actually do this work.

## FAQs

**1. Is Maulya available to use right now?**
Maulya's MVP is still under development. These pages explain what it is being built to do. Specific features will be confirmed as the product is released.

**2. Does Maulya calculate property value automatically?**
No. Maulya is not an automatic valuation engine. It is being built to organize the workflow around the valuer's work. The valuation opinion remains the valuer's.

**3. Is Maulya a borrower or buyer tool?**
No. Maulya is for valuation offices — valuers, firms, site engineers, and office staff. It is not a borrower-facing property valuation website.

**4. Is Maulya certified or required by any bank, RBI, or IBBI?**
No such claim is made. Maulya is a workflow platform. Compliance and professional responsibilities remain with the valuer and the office.

**5. Will Maulya replace my Word templates and Excel sheets entirely?**
Maulya is being designed to reduce reliance on scattered tools by keeping assignment information in one place. How much it replaces depends on the office workflow.
