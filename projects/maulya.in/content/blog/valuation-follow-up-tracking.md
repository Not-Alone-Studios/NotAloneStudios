The problem in valuation follow-up is not that people forget to call. It is that nobody remembers what happened after the last call.

A staff member calls a borrower on Monday. The borrower says, "Call me Thursday." By Thursday, the staff member is buried in three other files and the conversation has evaporated. Nobody is being careless. The detail simply had nowhere to live.

This is the quiet weakness in most valuation offices. The calling happens. The remembering doesn't.

## Follow-up is part of the work, not an interruption

It helps to start by accepting something: in bank valuation work, follow-up is not a distraction from the job. It *is* a large part of the job.

A site visit can't happen until the borrower is reached and a time is fixed. A report can't be finalised until pending documents arrive. A branch officer will call for status whether or not you have one ready. None of this is noise sitting on top of the "real" work. Moving a file forward *is* mostly follow-up.

Once you see it that way, the goal changes. The aim is not to reduce follow-up — that's not realistic. The aim is to stop losing the thread of it.

## The follow-ups that fill a valuation office's day

Most offices are running several kinds of follow-up at once, often without naming them:

- **Borrower contact for the site visit** — the most common, and the most frustrating. The borrower doesn't answer, or answers and asks to push the visit.
- **Alternate contact or key holder** — the property is locked, and a caretaker, tenant, or family member has the key.
- **Pending documents** — something is awaited from the borrower or the branch before the report can move.
- **Branch clarification** — a question about which property exactly, or whether it's the same one valued earlier.
- **Site engineer evidence** — the engineer visited, but the photos or measurements aren't in yet.
- **Valuer input** — something needs the valuer's decision before staff can proceed.
- **Report review and correction** — a draft is with the valuer, or corrections are pending with staff.
- **Submission confirmation** — confirming the report actually reached the branch.

Each of these is a thread. When a thread is dropped, it usually surfaces later as a surprised "wait, what happened with that one?"

## What to record for every follow-up

A follow-up that isn't written down is hard for the office to rely on tomorrow. For each one, a few details are worth capturing:

- **Date and time** of the contact
- **Who was contacted** — borrower, branch officer, key holder, engineer
- **Mode** — call, WhatsApp, email, or branch visit
- **What was asked**
- **What was promised** — and by whom
- **Next follow-up date**
- **Who owns it** — which person in the office is responsible for the next step
- **Status or result**

This is not bureaucracy. It is the difference between an office that can answer a question instantly and one that has to reconstruct events from three people's memories.

## Record "called, no answer" too

This one is slightly counterintuitive, so it's worth stating directly: an attempted call with no record looks exactly like no effort at all.

If a branch officer calls and the office can say, "We called the borrower on the 3rd, 5th, and 7th. On the 7th he agreed to Saturday, but the property was locked when our engineer reached," that is a clear, honest status. The delay is visible and the office looks on top of it.

If there is no record, the same office looks like it has been sitting on the file. The work may have been identical — the difference is entirely in whether the follow-up was captured.

## Know whose delay it is

Not every delay sits with the office, and it helps to be honest about which is which. Broadly, a stuck file is usually waiting on one of three sides:

- **Borrower-side** — site visit not yet possible, documents not provided, property inaccessible.
- **Branch-side** — clarification awaited, an input the branch was to provide.
- **Office-side** — engineer's evidence pending, draft pending, valuer review pending.

The point isn't to assign blame. It's that the office should know, at any moment, where a file is actually stuck — so it can act on the part it controls and give an accurate status on the part it doesn't. When a branch asks "why is this delayed," a vague answer erodes trust. A specific one, grounded in your own follow-up record, holds up.

## Don't let the branch's status call be your tracking system

Here is a habit worth breaking: in many offices, the only time a file gets looked at is when the branch calls to chase it. The branch call becomes the reminder system.

When that happens, the branch is effectively running your office's priorities. You're always reacting, never ahead. Follow-up tracking flips this. The office knows the status of a file before the branch asks — which means the conversation changes from "let me check and call you back" to "yes, we're waiting on the borrower, visit is set for Saturday." That small difference is what makes an office feel reliable.

## A simple follow-up tracker

You don't need a CRM to start. A single shared sheet or even a dedicated notebook works, as long as it's tied to the assignment and not floating in a chat.

One row per follow-up. Columns for the assignment reference, who was contacted, the mode, what was asked, what was promised, the next follow-up date, the owner, and the result. The key discipline is simple: the moment a call ends, the row gets updated — not later, not "when I get a minute." A follow-up tracker only works if it's updated while the conversation is still fresh.

## Practical Checklist: A Follow-up Record

For every follow-up — including the ones that go unanswered — capture:

- [ ] Assignment reference
- [ ] Date and time of contact
- [ ] Who was contacted (borrower / branch officer / key holder / engineer)
- [ ] Mode (call / WhatsApp / email / branch visit)
- [ ] What was asked
- [ ] What was promised, and by whom
- [ ] Outcome — including "called, no answer"
- [ ] Next follow-up date
- [ ] Who owns the next step

**One honest question to ask per file:** Is this delay borrower-side, branch-side, or office-side — and what's our next move on it?

## FAQs

**1. What is valuation follow-up tracking?**
It's the practice of recording every follow-up on a bank valuation assignment — borrower calls, branch queries, pending documents, evidence chasing — so the office always knows what was last asked, what was promised, who owns the next step, and when to check again.

**2. Why is follow-up such a big part of valuation work?**
Because a file can't move without it. Site visits depend on reaching the borrower, reports depend on pending documents arriving, and branches will ask for status regardless. Moving an assignment forward is largely a sequence of follow-ups.

**3. Should the office record calls that went unanswered?**
Yes. An attempted call with no record looks identical to no effort. Recording "called, no answer" lets the office give an honest, specific status instead of appearing to sit on the file.

**4. How can an office tell whose delay it is?**
By tracking where each file is actually waiting — borrower-side (visit or documents pending), branch-side (clarification awaited), or office-side (evidence, draft, or review pending). This isn't about blame; it's about knowing what the office can act on and giving accurate status on the rest.

**5. Do we need software to track follow-ups?**
No. A shared sheet or a dedicated notebook tied to the assignment works to start. The discipline that matters is updating it the moment a call ends, while the details are still fresh — not the tool itself.
