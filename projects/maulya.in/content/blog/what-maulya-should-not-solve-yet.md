A serious product earns trust not only by saying what it does, but also by being clear about what it does not do.

It is tempting, especially before launch, to suggest a product can solve everything. But valuation professionals have good instincts, and an overpromising tool loses credibility quickly. So this page is deliberately about limits — what Maulya should not try to solve yet, and why that restraint is a feature, not a weakness.

## Why honest boundaries build trust

A valuer's entire professional standing rests on careful, considered judgment. They are naturally sceptical of any tool that claims to remove difficulty from work that is genuinely difficult. If any early-stage product claimed to do everything, cautious professionals would be right to ask questions.

Clear boundaries do the opposite. When a product says plainly "this is what we focus on, and this is what we do not claim," it becomes easier to trust the things it does claim. Saying "not yet" can be a sign of seriousness, not a sign of weakness.

## What Maulya is not claiming at this stage

Here is an honest list of things Maulya is not trying to do at this stage:

- **Automatic property valuation** — Maulya is not deciding market value. Value is a matter of professional judgment, and that stays with the valuer.
- **Replacement for valuer judgment** — the tool organises work; it does not make valuation decisions.
- **Legal title verification** — Maulya does not confirm ownership, title, or the legal status of a property.
- **Compliance guarantees** — Maulya does not claim to make any office RBI-compliant, SBI-compliant, bank-compliant, or IBBI-compliant, and makes no claim about what any bank or authority requires.
- **Guaranteed bank acceptance** — Maulya cannot promise that a report will be accepted by any bank.
- **Fraud detection** — Maulya does not detect fraud, fake documents, or misrepresentation.
- **Automatic or flawless report generation** — Maulya should not claim that reports are produced perfectly on their own; inputs, review, and judgment remain with the valuer.
- **Every property type and bank format from day one** — valuation work varies widely, and Maulya is not claiming to handle every variation immediately.
- **Full finance, billing, CRM, or ERP replacement** — Maulya is a workflow tool, not an accounting or enterprise system.

This is not a list of failures. It is a list of honest boundaries for an MVP that is still under development.

## What Maulya should focus on first

Against that, here is the narrower job Maulya is actually being built to do well:

- **Assignment intake** — capturing the key details when an assignment arrives.
- **Site visit workflow** — tracking what has been visited and what is still pending.
- **Evidence upload** — keeping site photos and documents against the right assignment.
- **Valuation input capture** — recording the inputs a report will need, where relevant to the office process.
- **Report preparation status** — showing what is in progress, in review, and ready.
- **Valuer review and approval** — supporting the valuer's check before submission.
- **Final report storage** — keeping submitted reports retrievable later.
- **Assignment history** — making it easier to find a past assignment and its records when a query comes up later.

That is a focused, practical scope. It is about making the core valuation office workflow visible and trackable — not about replacing the profession's expertise.

## Why focus matters for an MVP

A product that tries to do everything at once often struggles to do the core things well. For something still under development, focus is the difference between a tool that genuinely helps and a bloated system nobody adopts. By concentrating on workflow visibility first, Maulya can be useful early and improve steadily — rather than promising a long feature list it cannot yet support. A stable foundation is worth more than an impressive-sounding one.

## Why "not yet" is better than overpromising

There is a real cost to overpromising. A valuer who adopts a tool expecting it to guarantee compliance, decide value, or catch every error will feel let down — and that lost trust is hard to recover. Saying "not yet" sets honest expectations, and honest expectations are what allow a professional to rely on a tool at all. It is better to do a few things dependably than to claim many things vaguely.

## Honesty is part of the product

For a profession built on careful judgment, the way a tool talks about itself matters as much as what it does. A product that is upfront about its limits is showing the same discipline a good valuation office shows in its own work — claiming only what can be supported, and being clear about the rest. That discipline is part of what Maulya is trying to be, not a disclaimer bolted on at the end.

## What can come later

Once the workflow foundation is stable and genuinely useful, there is room to grow. But that growth has to be earned by getting the basics right first — and this page deliberately does not make roadmap promises. The order matters: foundation first, expansion later, and only when the core is solid.

## An honest closing

Maulya's first job is not to solve every valuation problem. Its first job is to make the core valuation office workflow visible, trackable, and easier to manage. If it does that honestly and well, it earns the right to do more. That is the standard it is being built to.

## Practical Summary

- A serious product is clear about what it **does not** do — that clarity builds trust.
- **Maulya does not claim (yet):** automatic valuation, replacement for judgment, title verification, compliance guarantees, guaranteed bank acceptance, fraud detection, perfect reports, every format from day one, or finance/CRM/ERP functions.
- **Maulya focuses first on:** intake, site visit workflow, evidence upload, input capture, report status, valuer review, final storage, and assignment history.
- **Focus is the point** for an MVP — a stable foundation beats a long feature list.
- "Not yet" sets honest expectations; honest expectations make it easier for professionals to judge whether a tool fits their work.
- Future scope is conditional on getting the core right — no roadmap promises here.

## FAQs

**Q1. Does Maulya calculate or decide the property value?**
No. Maulya does not decide value. It is being built to organise and track the workflow around an assignment. The valuation itself — rates, approach, and final figures — remains the valuer's professional judgment.

**Q2. Will Maulya make my reports compliant or guarantee the bank accepts them?**
No. Maulya makes no compliance claims and cannot guarantee acceptance by any bank or authority. It is being built to help manage the workflow, not to certify the report or the office.

**Q3. Can Maulya verify title or detect fraud?**
No. Maulya does not verify ownership or title and does not detect fraud or fake documents. Those remain matters of professional checking and judgment outside the tool's scope.

**Q4. Why not just build everything at once?**
Because a tool that tries to do everything often struggles to do the core things well — especially at the MVP stage. Focusing on workflow visibility first lets Maulya be genuinely useful early and improve on a stable foundation.

**Q5. So what *can* Maulya actually help with right now?**
Its intended focus is the core office workflow: capturing assignment details, tracking site visits, organising evidence, recording inputs, showing report status, supporting valuer review, and storing final reports so past work is easier to retrieve later.
