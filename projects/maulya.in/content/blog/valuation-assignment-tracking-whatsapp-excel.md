# Why Valuation Offices Still Run on WhatsApp and Excel — and What It Quietly Costs

Most valuation offices in India never chose a system. They grew into one.

A WhatsApp chat for bank assignments. An Excel sheet for tracking. A phone gallery full of site photos. A desktop folder for finished reports. None of it was a decision — it was simply what was within reach when the work started coming in. And to be clear, this is not about valuers being careless. Many sharp, hard-working offices run almost entirely this way.

It works well for a long time. But as the number of assignments grows, the first thing that quietly breaks is valuation assignment tracking — the ability to say, at any moment, where each file stands and who is handling it.

## How a bank valuation assignment actually moves through the office

A single assignment usually travels through a long chain of small steps.

A bank branch sends the assignment — often by WhatsApp, sometimes by a call or email — for a property being mortgaged. Someone notes down the borrower's (and often a co-borrower's) contact number. The property then has to be located: not just an address, but a landmark, and the locality, village, taluk, or district that actually helps someone find it on the ground.

Depending on how the office works, the valuer, assistant, or site engineer visits the property. They take property photos and photograph the available documents. Those photos usually come back to the office on WhatsApp. Office staff then prepare the report, most often in a Word template. The valuer does a final review before the report is printed, emailed, uploaded, or submitted as per the office's normal process. After that come the follow-ups — the branch or the borrower asking whether it's done.

This is a real, working method. It has carried many practices for years.

## Why this works when assignment volume is low

At a small number of assignments, this setup is enough — because one person is quietly holding the whole picture in their head.

You remember which file is at which stage. The photos in the gallery are recent, so they're easy to find. Follow-ups are rare, and you can answer them from memory. Excel works because you're usually the only one updating it, so there's no confusion about which version is current.

At this scale, the tools are not the system. You are.

## Where valuation assignment tracking starts to break

The shift is never dramatic, which is exactly why it's easy to miss.

More bank branches start sending work. Assignments increase. A second person joins, or a site engineer begins handling visits. Two or three site visits happen on the same day. Several Word reports are being drafted at once.

Nothing about the tools has changed. But the picture no longer fits inside one person's head — and from that point, the same WhatsApp and Excel setup starts behaving very differently.

## The quiet leakage points

These problems rarely appear as one big mistake. They show up as small frictions that repeat.

- **Borrower or co-borrower number not noted properly.** The site visit can't be fixed until someone digs through old messages, and a day is lost.
- **Address fine, but landmark or locality unclear.** The site engineer reaches the wrong lane or the wrong village, calls back confused, and half a trip is wasted.
- **Site photos and document photos buried in a WhatsApp scroll.** Property, frontage, and site-condition photos, along with the document photos, end up mixed with dozens of other properties — slow to find, with a quiet risk of attaching the wrong property's photos.
- **Staff re-asking the valuer the same details.** Because the information sits in someone's head or in scattered chats, the valuer gets interrupted again and again.
- **No clarity on who has the file.** Two people each assume the other is handling it, and the assignment simply sits still.
- **A report stuck, noticed too late.** It often surfaces only when the branch calls to ask where it is.
- **A report prepared but not reviewed.** In the rush, the valuer's final review gets skipped, because nothing clearly marked that step as pending.
- **An old report that's hard to retrieve.** When a branch asks for a clarification on an earlier valuation, or the same property comes up again, finding the file becomes a hunt through folders and chats.

Any one of these is minor on its own. That's the trap. The cost is quiet — it looks like delays, repeated work, and a low background level of stress the office just gets used to.

## The real problem is workflow visibility

This is the part worth sitting with.

WhatsApp was built for messaging. Excel was built for calculation. Both are excellent at what they were designed for. But neither was built to answer the one question a busy valuation office needs answered all day:

*Where does each assignment stand right now, and who is responsible for the next step?*

That is a visibility gap, not a discipline gap. An experienced, careful office can still lose track when the information is spread across chats, sheets, galleries, and folders that don't talk to each other.

## What every valuation office should track clearly

You don't need anything fancy to close that gap. You need a single, current view of a few things for every assignment.

Every open file should have two things clearly visible: its current stage and the person responsible for the next step. The details worth tracking:

- **Assignment received date** — when the branch sent it.
- **Bank / branch** — who it's for.
- **Borrower (and co-borrower) contact** — captured cleanly, every time.
- **Property address / landmark** — including locality, village, taluk, or district.
- **Site visit status** — scheduled, done, or pending.
- **Responsible person** — one clear owner for the file.
- **Documents / photos pending** — what's still missing.
- **Valuation inputs pending** — what the valuer still needs to decide.
- **Report draft status** — not started, in draft, or ready.
- **Valuer review status** — reviewed and signed off, or not yet.
- **Final report / file location** — where it's stored, so it's easy to find later.

None of this changes the valuation work itself; that judgment stays with the valuer. It only makes the steps you already follow visible — so the office stops running on memory.

## A quick test for your own office

Try answering these right now, without checking anything:

- Which assignments are pending a site visit?
- Who is holding each open file at this moment?
- Was there a clear final review before each report left the office?
- Where is a report you sent eight months ago?

If you answered all four quickly, your current setup is working — keep it. If you hesitated on even one, the issue probably isn't how hard your office works.

## FAQs

**Is there anything wrong with running an office on WhatsApp and Excel?**
No. They're useful and familiar, and at low volume they work well. The strain usually begins only as assignments, staff, and site visits increase.

**How do I know I've outgrown this setup?**
A quick test: can you say which files are pending a site visit and who holds each one — without checking anything? If you hesitate, it may be a tracking issue rather than an effort one.

**What's the most common point where things slip?**
Unclear ownership of a file. When two people each assume the other is handling it, the assignment quietly stalls until someone follows up.

**Do I need software to fix this?**
Not first. Start by logging every assignment the same way and giving each file one clear owner. Software helps once manual tracking can't keep up with the volume.

**Will tracking change how I value a property?**
No. The valuation and the judgment stay entirely with the valuer. Only the surrounding workflow — status, pending items, retrieval — becomes easier to see.
