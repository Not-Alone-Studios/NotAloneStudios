## No. And that is deliberate.

No. Maulya does not automatically decide property value — and that is deliberate.

It is worth stating plainly at the top, because "AI that values property" is a claim floating around the market, and serious professionals are right to ask careful questions about it. Maulya is not being built to produce a value figure on its own. It is being built to organize the work *around* the valuation — so the valuer's judgment has a clean place to live, not a machine standing in for it.

## Why this distinction matters

A bank valuation is a professional opinion, formed by a qualified valuer, based on inspection, evidence, method, and judgment. It carries the valuer's name and responsibility. That is not a detail to automate away.

If a tool quietly suggested it could decide the value, it would be making a promise it cannot keep and putting the valuer's professional standing at risk in the process. Maulya avoids that line entirely. The value is the valuer's to determine. Maulya's job is everything around that determination.

## What Maulya can help organize

Here is the honest scope of what Maulya is being designed to do — none of it is "deciding the value."

It can help organize **assignment intake**, so a new file starts in one place. It can hold **site visit details** and keep **photos and documents** attached to the right assignment. It can keep **property observations** with the file. It can hold the **valuation inputs that the valuer records** — the figures and notes are entered by the valuer, not generated by the system. It can support the **report preparation workflow**, the **corrections**, the **final approval** step, and **storage and retrieval** so a completed file can be found later.

Read that list again and notice what is missing: at no point does the system form an opinion on what the property is worth. It holds, organizes, and moves the work. The thinking stays human.

## What remains entirely with the valuer

Everything that actually constitutes the valuation stays with the valuer:

- the inspection and what it reveals;
- the choice of method or approach;
- the rate assumptions and the reasoning behind them;
- the adjustments for the specific property and its situation;
- the final valuation opinion;
- and the review and sign-off on the report.

Maulya is being built to make these steps easier to *carry out and keep on record* — not to perform them. The line is simple: the system handles the workflow, the valuer handles the valuation.

## Why automatic valuation claims need careful review

When a product implies that software can produce a professional valuation opinion on its own, it creates a problem for the very professional using it. A valuation output that a valuer cannot fully stand behind is not a useful shortcut, because the responsibility does not move to the software — it stays with the person who signed.

Good judgment in this field is built on inspection and context that a generic engine does not have. Treating value as something a tool can output, untouched by the valuer, is exactly the kind of claim a serious office should distrust. Maulya is being built for people who already know this — which is why it does not make that claim.

## Why workflow still matters when the judgment is human

Here is the point that sometimes gets missed: deciding *not* to automate the valuation does not mean there is nothing to improve. The judgment being human is precisely why the workflow around it deserves to be clean.

A valuer's opinion is only as good as the evidence and inputs in front of them when they form it. If site photos are scattered, if a document is missing and no one flagged it, if the wrong file is open, the judgment is being made on a shaky base. Organized intake, attached evidence, recorded inputs, a visible review step — these support the quality of a human decision. That is where Maulya is being built to help, and it is a real help even though it touches none of the valuation itself.

## How Maulya supports valuation practice, rather than replacing it

The cleanest way to hold it in mind: Maulya is being built to remove avoidable confusion *around* the valuer, not to remove the valuer from the valuation.

It is the assistant that keeps the file in order, surfaces what is pending, and stores the finished work — so the valuer can spend attention on the parts that genuinely need professional judgment. Support, not substitution. The valuer remains responsible for professional judgment throughout.

## A clear closing

So, does Maulya automatically decide property value? No.

The point of Maulya is not to remove the valuer from valuation. The point is to remove avoidable workflow confusion around the valuer — and to do it honestly, without pretending a tool can carry a responsibility that belongs to a qualified professional.

## Practical summary

- Maulya does **not** calculate, estimate, or decide property value.
- It organizes the workflow: intake, evidence, inputs (entered by the valuer), report prep, corrections, approval, and storage.
- Inspection, method, rates, adjustments, the final opinion, and sign-off stay with the valuer.
- "AI valuation" claims are risky because responsibility stays with the person who signs.
- Clean workflow supports the quality of a human judgment — which is why it still matters.
