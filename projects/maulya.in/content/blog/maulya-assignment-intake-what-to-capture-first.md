A valuation assignment should not begin as a half-remembered WhatsApp message.

But in a lot of valuation offices, that is exactly how it starts. A branch officer messages the valuer, or the valuer's number gets shared with the borrower, and the first "record" of the assignment is a forwarded chat with a property address, a phone number, and the words "please do the needful." Three days later, when office staff sit down to plan the site visit, half the details are missing and someone has to call the branch again to confirm what was already discussed.

Assignment intake is the moment a new bank valuation assignment enters your office and becomes a tracked piece of work. It is the first checkpoint — and it is where a surprising number of delays quietly begin.

This page explains how Maulya is being built to handle assignment intake, what details it is designed to capture first, and just as importantly, what it does not do.

## Why intake is where delays begin

When intake is informal, three things tend to happen.

The same questions get asked twice. The borrower's alternate number, the exact landmark, whether the approval copy was received — these get re-confirmed over multiple calls because nobody wrote them down in one place the first time.

Site visits get planned with incomplete information. The site engineer reaches the locality and then calls back to ask for the nearest landmark or the contact person, because the address in the message was not enough to actually find the property.

Handover becomes guesswork. When the assignment moves from whoever received it to whoever prepares the report, context is lost. The person preparing the draft has to reconstruct what the assignment even is.

None of this is a skill problem. It is a capture problem. The details existed at some point — usually in a phone call — but they were never recorded in a structured place.

## What Maulya is being built to capture at intake

Maulya is being built to help offices record new assignment details in one structured place at the point of intake, instead of leaving them scattered across chats and memory. Depending on the office workflow and available product scope, the intake details Maulya is designed to capture include:

- **Assignment reference** — so every later step can point back to one file
- **Bank and branch** — which institution and which branch the work came from
- **Borrower and co-borrower details** — names as shared by the branch
- **Contact number and an alternate contact** — so a single unreachable number does not stall the visit
- **Property address, landmark, and locality / village / town** — enough to actually reach the site
- **Taluk, district, and state**
- **Property type** — land, building, flat, and so on
- **Purpose, where available**
- **Due date or expected timeline**
- **Assigned person** — who in the office owns this assignment
- **Documents received and documents pending** — so gaps are visible from day one
- **Notes and special instructions** — anything the branch flagged verbally

The point is not to fill every field for the sake of it. The point is that the details which usually go missing have a defined place to live.

## What Maulya does not do at intake

It is worth being clear about the limits, because honest limits build more trust than inflated claims.

At intake, Maulya does not decide the valuation. Capturing a property's details is not the same as valuing it, and Maulya is not built to produce a value from intake data.

It does not verify legal title, ownership, or approvals automatically. Recording that a document was received is a workflow note, not a legal check. Verifying what those documents actually say remains the valuer's professional responsibility.

It does not replace checking documents. A field marked "received" tells you the office has the file — not that the file is correct or complete.

And it does not make every bank follow the same intake format. Different banks and branches share work differently. Maulya is being built to give your office a consistent internal structure, not to standardise how banks send assignments.

## Bad intake vs structured intake

Here is the difference in practice.

**Unstructured intake:** A branch shares a borrower's number on WhatsApp with a one-line property description. The valuer forwards it to a site engineer. Two days later the engineer can't locate the property and calls the office. The office doesn't have a landmark either, so they call the branch. The branch is busy. The visit slips by a day. Nobody is at fault, but a day is gone.

**Structured intake:** The same assignment is entered once — reference, branch, borrower, two contact numbers, address with landmark and locality, property type, due date, and a note that the approval copy is still pending. The site engineer opens one file and has everything needed to plan the visit. The pending approval copy is visible to everyone, so it gets chased early instead of discovered late.

Same work. Same people. The only thing that changed is that the details were captured cleanly at the start.

This is also why intake connects directly to the rest of the workflow. A clean intake feeds a cleaner site visit, a cleaner handover, and a cleaner report.

The goal of Maulya's intake workflow is simple: capture the details early so every later step has a cleaner starting point.

## Practical summary

- Intake is the first checkpoint of an assignment — and a common starting point for delays.
- Most intake problems are capture problems, not skill problems: the details existed but were never written down in one place.
- Maulya is being built to capture reference, bank/branch, borrower and contacts, full address with landmark and locality, property type, due date, assigned person, document status, and notes — depending on the workflow.
- Maulya does **not** value the property, verify title, validate documents, or standardise how banks send work.
- Structured intake can reduce repeat calls, support better site-visit planning, and keep handover cleaner.

## FAQs

**Q1. Is Maulya's assignment intake feature available now?**
Maulya's MVP is still under development. Intake is one of the workflows Maulya is being built around, and these pages describe how it is intended to work rather than claiming every part is already live.

**Q2. Does Maulya check whether the documents at intake are valid or complete?**
No. Recording a document as "received" or "pending" is a workflow note for the office. Checking what a document actually contains, and whether it is valid, remains the valuer's professional responsibility.

**Q3. Does capturing intake details mean Maulya starts valuing the property?**
No. Intake captures the details needed to plan and prepare the assignment. It does not produce a value. Valuation stays with the valuer at the appropriate stage.

**Q4. Do all banks need the same intake fields?**
No, and Maulya does not claim to standardise that. Banks and branches share work differently. Maulya is being built to give your office a consistent internal structure for whatever the bank sends.

**Q5. Can junior staff handle intake in Maulya?**
The intent is that whoever receives a new assignment — including office or junior staff — can record it in a structured way, so the details don't depend on one person's memory. The valuer still owns the professional decisions that follow.
