No. Maulya is not being built only for large valuation firms — because the workflow pain often starts much earlier.

There is a common assumption that workflow software is something you adopt only once an office is "big enough." A large firm with several valuers and multiple branches obviously needs systems. But if you look closely at where valuation work actually becomes messy, it is often the *small* office that feels the strain first — because there is no slack anywhere in the system.

## Why small offices feel the pain first

In a small valuation office, one person usually carries most of the load in their head. The valuer remembers which bank sent which assignment, which site visit is pending, which report is half-finished, and which branch is waiting for a reply. The office staff member — if there is one — tracks files across a notebook, an Excel sheet, and a phone gallery. Site photos arrive over WhatsApp and sit there until someone copies them out. Branch managers and borrowers call the valuer directly, often during a site visit or while a report is being finalised.

None of this is a sign of poor work. It is simply what happens when output grows faster than structure. A large firm may have more room to add coordination support. A small office usually cannot — so the pressure lands on the one or two people doing everything. That is exactly why Maulya is being designed with small offices in mind, not as an afterthought.

## What a solo valuer actually needs

A solo valuer is not looking for a corporate dashboard with twenty modules. The needs are simpler and more immediate:

- **Assignment tracking** — a clear list of which assignments are open, and where each one stands.
- **Site visit status** — what has been visited, what is scheduled, what is still pending.
- **Evidence organisation** — site photos and documents kept against the right assignment instead of scattered across a phone gallery.
- **Report review** — a way to check inputs before a report goes out.
- **Retrieval** — finding a past assignment and its records months later without digging through folders.

Maulya is being built to support exactly these steps, so a solo valuer can see their own pending work clearly rather than holding all of it in memory.

## What a small team needs

The moment a second or third person joins — a site engineer, an office staff member — a new question appears: who has the file right now, and what is the next step?

In a small team, that question is usually answered by calling each other. "Did you visit that site?" "Is the report ready?" "Did the branch send the documents?" These calls are not a problem occasionally. They become a problem when they happen all day.

Maulya is intended to make this shared visibility easier: the valuer, the site engineer, and the office staff can see what is pending, who owns the next step, and where each assignment stands — without a constant chain of follow-up messages. Field observations stay attributed to the site engineer, while valuation judgment — rates, approach, and final figures — remains with the valuer.

## What Maulya should not become

This is important, and it shapes how the product is being built. Maulya is not trying to become:

- A heavy enterprise ERP that takes months to roll out.
- A complicated corporate system that needs training sessions before anyone can do anything.
- A generic CRM bolted onto valuation work.

A small valuation office does not need any of that. Adding a heavy system to a small team usually creates more work than it removes, which defeats the entire purpose.

## Why starting small matters

The honest truth is that visibility is more valuable than complexity. Knowing what is pending, who owns it, and where each file stands solves a large share of everyday friction. Most small offices do not need automation first — they need clarity first. So Maulya is being built to start narrow: capture the assignment, track the workflow, organise the evidence, and help the valuer review before submission. That foundation matters more than a long feature list.

## Which offices may benefit first

Maulya is likely to help most where:

- One or two people are carrying assignment status in memory.
- Site photos and documents are scattered across WhatsApp and folders.
- Branch and borrower follow-ups land directly on the valuer.
- Past assignments are hard to retrieve when needed later.

If that describes your office, the size of the firm is not the deciding factor. The workflow pressure is.

## What Maulya is still not promising

To be clear and honest about the current stage:

- Maulya is not deciding property value automatically — the valuer remains responsible for professional judgment.
- Maulya is not a compliance guarantee for any bank or authority.
- Maulya is not a replacement for the valuer's review and sign-off.
- Maulya is still under development, not a fully mature enterprise system.

The goal is not to make a small office look like a large corporate one. The goal is to help even a small valuation office see what is pending, who owns the next step, and where each assignment stands.

- Maulya is **not** only for large firms — workflow pain often hits small offices hardest because everything sits in one or two people's memory.
- A **solo valuer** benefits from assignment tracking, site visit status, evidence organisation, report review, and retrieval.

## FAQs

**Q1. I am a solo valuer. Is Maulya overkill for me?**
It is being built with solo valuers in mind, so the aim is that it should not feel like overkill. A solo valuer can use it simply — mainly to keep open assignments, site visit status, and evidence in one visible place instead of in memory. You are not required to use every part of it.

**Q2. Do I need a team for Maulya to be useful?**
No. Shared visibility helps when a site engineer or office staff member is involved, but a single valuer still benefits from clear assignment tracking and easier retrieval of past work.

**Q3. Will this turn my small office into a complicated corporate setup?**
That is specifically what it is being designed to avoid. The aim is clarity, not complexity. Maulya is not intended to become a heavy ERP or force corporate processes on a small office.

**Q4. Does Maulya decide the property value for me?**
No. Maulya does not decide value. It is being built to help organise and track the workflow around an assignment. The valuation judgment — rates, approach, and final figures — remains entirely with the valuer.
