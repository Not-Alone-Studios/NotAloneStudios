Product overview

Maulya helps valuation firms run the work behind the report.

Maulya gives valuation firms a role-aware workspace for assignment flow, evidence capture, approvals, assisted report generation, billing visibility, and final release. This page reflects the current operating model in practical terms.

Why this matters

The platform is designed so older and non-technical users can still tell what is blocked, what is approved, and what to do next.

01Onboard

Tenant state, company details, approval posture, and user roles shape what the workspace exposes.

02Assign

03Evidence

06Release

Assignments and evidence

Queues are the product, not just the wrapper.

Maulya is strongest when the assignment stops being a long detail page and becomes a real workspace: queues, previews, warnings, ownership, evidence, and next actions in one surface.

  • Assignment flow designed for operators, reviewers, and field users
  • Evidence capture and readiness surfaced before release
  • Mobile execution subset for on-the-go actions instead of desktop-only assumptions
Field capture

Draft-first creation and uploads

The mobile lane favors safer capture, uploads, and follow-up instead of pretending complex desk flows translate cleanly to phone screens.

Approvals

Blocked states should explain themselves

The product direction is explicit about why something is blocked: role, workflow, approval, plan, add-on, quota, or credit posture.

Billing visibility

Commercial truth belongs inside the workflow

Billing and credits should appear where users make report and release decisions, not only inside a separate admin lane.

Repogen

Assisted report generation without pretending it is simple.

Repogen is not marketed here as magic. It is positioned as an assisted reporting lane with processing, review, regeneration, release, and commercial implications.

  • Tenant-side commissioning and clearer processing surfaces
  • Approval and finalization tied to capability truth
  • Future tariff-aware charging instead of flat beta-era claims
Governance

Studio and tenant roles stay visible but distinct.

Core tenant, approved tenant, operators, reviewers, tenant admins, associates, and studio/governance users do not need the same UI or language. The public site now reflects that instead of flattening everyone into one generic story.

  • Default-deny report generation until approval gates clear
  • Studio oversight kept separate from tenant-side work
  • Associate surface intentionally narrower than internal workflow surfaces

What Maulya prevents

Operational drift across intake, upload, approval, invoice follow-up, and release. The platform is most useful where teams currently lose time to unclear ownership and quiet backlog growth.

What Maulya clarifies

Whether the next blocker is workflow state, capability state, billing posture, or tenant approval state. The public marketing story now matches that more mature truth.
