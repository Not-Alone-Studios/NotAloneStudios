The product

One file, from intake to hand-over.

Maulya is a web app for the whole office, on a computer at the desk and on a phone at the site. Here is what is on each screen today, in the app's own words.

Apply for early accessHow a file moves

Home: what needs you, first.

Each person's Home opens on "Needs your attention", with readouts for their own work and the files longest waiting first. Each row says where the file is, for how long, and the one thing to do: approve the report, give the file to someone, start the valuation.

  • With you, waiting at desk, visits today, ready to go.
  • Payments late, for those who see the money.
  • "Waiting at the desk", with why each file is there.

The Queue: every file, by where it is.

Tabs across the top count the files in each place: given to you, with you, waiting at desk, at desk, at site, not started, ready to go and finished. Each row has the file's reference, the borrower, the bank branch and the kind of property, how long it has been there and its next step.

A file's page: the whole story in one place.

The top of the file is a scale of where the work stands, with the date of each step. Beside it: who has the file now, since when, and what is next.

  • Overview, property, photos and documents, valuation, activity, report and finance.
  • Documents: which have come, which are missing, with an Upload button on each missing one.
  • The site visit: who to meet, the photos that have come.

A new file asks only what you know.

Who sent it, the property and the borrower, where it is, who does the work. The form counts what is filled, lets you save a draft, and shows the documents that bank will need once you choose the bank and the kind of property.

How a file moves

The seven places a file can be, with who works there.

1 of 7, Not started

The file comes in

The file is made once: who sent it (a bank, a client directly or another valuer), the bank and branch, the property and the borrower, where it is, and who does the work. You can save a draft and come back to it.

On the screen: "Documents this bank will need", "Choose the bank and the kind of property to see the documents this bank will need."

  • Owner
  • Associate valuer
  • Assistant valuer
  • Office staff

2 of 7, At site

The site visit

The site engineer sees the visit on the phone: when and where, who to meet, the site's exact location, and a check of the site in steps. Photos are taken against the list the report needs. When the visit is done, the phone asks who should check it.

On the screen: "Photos for the report", "Take each photo the report needs. Each one is kept on this phone first, then goes to the office by itself."

  • Site engineer
  • Assistant valuer

3 of 7, Waiting at desk

The evidence arrives

Photos and documents land on the file, not in someone's gallery. The file says what has come and what is still missing, so the office can ask for it while the field person can still be reached. The owner or an assistant valuer checks the visit: accepts it, or sends it back to the site engineer.

On the screen: "Documents", "3 of 5 in. 2 missing."

  • Owner
  • Assistant valuer
  • Office staff

4 of 7, At desk

The report is made

The desk makes the report on the report form, from what is on the file: the property, the photos and the documents. Office staff can fill the whole form; the prevailing rate and the adopted rate stay with the valuer. The file shows who has it and since when.

On the screen: "Where the file is now", "At desk. Report being written."

  • Office staff
  • Assistant valuer

5 of 7, With the valuer

The valuer checks and approves

The report comes to the valuer to check. Approving it is the valuer's step, never assumed: one press approves it and asks who prints, signs and hands it over. Nobody approves their own report, and office staff never approve.

In the app, the equals mark levels when a registered valuer finalises.

On the screen: "The report is ready to check", "Approve the report"

  • Owner
  • Associate valuer
  • Assistant valuer

6 of 7, Ready to go

Printed, signed and handed over

The person the valuer chose prints the report, signs it and hands it over, marking each step on the file. The file shows what is left, such as signing the printed report. At hand-over the payment is recorded on the file.

On the screen: "Ready to go", "Sign the printed report"

  • Owner
  • Associate valuer
  • Office staff

7 of 7, Delivered

Handed over, and kept

When the report is handed over and nothing is owed, the file closes by itself. The finished report stays with its file: the photos, the documents and the history of who did what. Months later, when a bank or borrower asks about an old report, it can be found.

On the screen: "Files you worked on", "Finished"

  • Everyone who worked on it
The phone

Built for the site, and for poor signal.

Maulya runs in the phone's browser today, for every job: Home, files, reports and the site visit. The site engineer's day starts with the visits waiting for a yes, each with Accept and Decline.

  • Each photo is kept on the phone first, then goes to the office by itself.
  • "As it was at 7:52 am. Pull down to get the newest." The phone says how fresh its page is.
  • A new file can be started from the phone.

In beta The calmer phone design in these pictures is in beta and not yet switched on for every firm.

Reports and fees

Reports in the bank's format. Fees beside the work.

A report is counted once

"A report is counted the first time it is made. Making it again in a new version costs nothing."

Borrower and bank stay fixed

After the first report, the borrower's name and the bank can't be changed on that file, so a version can't drift.

Finance, for those allowed

Invoices, payments and what is late, on the file and in Finance. A person without the Finance switch sees no fee, no invoice and no money anywhere.

The office's own records

Who did what and when, kept in each file's activity and the firm's audit history.
